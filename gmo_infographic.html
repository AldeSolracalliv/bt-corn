02 — Background
What Is Bt Corn?

Bt corn is a genetically modified maize engineered to produce a protein from Bacillus thuringiensis — a built-in insecticide against the Asiatic corn borer (Ostrinia furnacalis), the most destructive corn pest in the Philippines. It was commercially approved in 2002, making the Philippines the first country in Southeast Asia to greenlight a GM food crop.


Today, Bt corn and herbicide-tolerant (HT) corn dominate production across Mindanao — the country's primary corn basket — with stacked BtHT varieties increasingly common.

03 — The Core Issue
The Dependency Cycle

While GMO corn initially promised higher yields, the shift has locked Filipino smallholder farmers into a costly cycle. Seeds owned by multinationals (Monsanto/Bayer, Syngenta) cannot legally be saved or replanted — a new purchase is required every season. Combined with mandatory herbicide costs for HT varieties, input costs have risen sharply for farmers already operating on thin margins.

05 — Farm-Level Income Study
Does GMO Corn Actually Pay Off?

A farm-level study in Isabela found no statistically significant difference in net income, production output, or production-cost ratio between non-GM, Bt, HT, and BtHT corn growers. The results challenge the premise that adopting GMO corn provides a meaningful economic advantage.

How Farmers Are Affected
💸
Rising Input Costs

09 — Proposed Reforms
Paths Forward
1
Strengthen seed sovereignty legislation — ensure farmers' legal right to save, replant, and exchange traditional varieties without corporate penalty.
2
Post-release monitoring by DA — the Department of Agriculture must conduct systematic post-release monitoring and glyphosate residue testing of GM corn fields and surrounding communities.
3
Support for farmers shifting away from GM corn — provide non-GM corn seeds and promote diversified, agroecological farming for farmers wanting to transition out of GMO dependency.
4
Fund community OPV seed banks — expand government support for open-pollinated variety seed banks and community seed libraries in corn-producing provinces.
5
Farmer participation in policy — the 2015 ban controversy shows the urgent need to involve farmers in regulatory discourse. Satisfaction and lived experience must inform GMO regulation.
6
Integrate indigenous & agroecological knowledge — MASIPAG advocates for farmer-led sustainable agriculture as an alternative to capital and chemical-intensive farming that GM corn has reinforced.
